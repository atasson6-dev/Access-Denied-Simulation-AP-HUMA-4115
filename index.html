Patient 01 · Housing insecurity

Meet Jordan

Jordan is a Black man experiencing homelessness. He needs to book an appointment but has limited mobile data, no stable mailing address, and unreliable access to a phone.

Explore how identity verification, digital access and hypothetical scheduling algorithms can create obstacles.

👵

Patient 02 · Digital exclusion

Meet Margaret

Margaret is an older adult who doesn't own a smartphone or computer. Her clinic encourages patients to book appointments through its online portal.

Explore device requirements, digital literacy, verification and access to human support.

Important: This is a fictional educational simulation, not a functioning healthcare booking service. It illustrates plausible access barriers; it does not depict a specific Canadian clinic or claim that all platforms use AI.

Behind the booking screen

Who is responsible when a patient cannot complete a booking? The healthcare organization choosing the system, the software vendor designing it, administrators configuring access rules, and clinicians responsible for safe pathways to care all have roles. Responsibility depends on the actual system and circumstances; it cannot simply be assigned to an algorithm.
