Behind the experience

What barriers does the research identify?

AI can reinforce scheduling inequalities

AI-assisted scheduling can reproduce racial inequalities when algorithms rely on biased historical data. This raises concerns about fair appointment access for marginalized patients (Shanklin et al.).

Beyond the screen

Who is responsible for these barriers?

Clinics and healthcare organizations

Choose booking systems carefully, offer telephone and in-person alternatives, train staff, and respond when patients cannot complete an online booking.

Platform designers and developers

Test with diverse patients, reduce unnecessary fields, use readable language, support assistive technology, and provide clear ways to request help.

Clinicians

Recognize when digital barriers delay care, help patients find other booking routes, and report repeated problems to clinic leadership.

Health system leaders

Monitor who is and is not using online booking, assess equity impacts, and ensure digital services do not become the only way to access care.

AI and unequal access: AI-assisted scheduling can reinforce existing bias. For marginalized patients, rigid booking requirements and limited human support can add barriers to care.
